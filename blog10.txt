# What Is a Free Kick in Football? A Simple Guide to the Rules

Free kicks are one of the most common ways play restarts in football. They can happen after fouls, handball offenses, and certain other violations of the Laws of the Game.

But what exactly is a free kick, and what is the difference between a direct and indirect free kick? Here’s a simple guide.

## What Is a Free Kick?

A free kick is a restart awarded to a team after an offense has been committed.

The ball is placed at the location where the offense occurred, subject to the specific rules for that situation. The opposing team must usually stay the required distance away from the ball while the kick is taken.

There are two main types:

* **Direct free kick**
* **Indirect free kick**

## What Is a Direct Free Kick?

A direct free kick allows the player taking the kick to score directly.

This means the ball can go straight into the opponent's goal from the free kick and count as a goal, provided all the relevant requirements are met.

Direct free kicks are commonly awarded for offenses such as certain types of:

* Kicking or attempting to kick an opponent
* Tripping or attempting to trip an opponent
* Pushing
* Holding
* Careless, reckless, or excessive physical challenges
* Certain handball offenses

## What Is an Indirect Free Kick?

An indirect free kick is different because the ball must touch another player before a goal can be scored.

If an indirect free kick goes directly into the opponent's goal without touching another player, the goal does not count. Play is restarted with a goal kick.

The referee normally indicates an indirect free kick by raising one arm and keeping it raised until another player touches the ball or it goes out of play.

## How Far Away Must Opponents Be?

When a free kick is taken, opponents generally have to stay at least **9.15 meters (10 yards)** from the ball.

There are some situations where the rules provide exceptions, particularly when a team takes a quick free kick or when the kick is taken from inside the team's own penalty area.

## Can You Take a Free Kick Quickly?

Yes. A team does not always have to wait for the defending team to organize itself.

A player can sometimes take a quick free kick to restart play and catch the opposition off guard.

However, if the referee needs to manage the required distance or deal with another situation before the kick, the restart may need to wait.

## What Happens if the Free Kick Goes Into the Goal?

The result depends on the type of free kick.

### Direct Free Kick

A direct free kick can go straight into the opponent's goal and count.

### Indirect Free Kick

The ball must touch another player before entering the opponent's goal for the goal to count.

Understanding this difference makes many free-kick situations much easier to follow.

## Free Kicks Near the Penalty Area

Free kicks close to the penalty area can create some of football's most exciting moments.

Teams may try to:

* Shoot directly
* Cross the ball into the penalty area
* Pass to a teammate
* Use a rehearsed set-piece routine

Defenders often form a **wall** to make it harder for the attacking player to shoot directly at goal.

## Why Are Free Kicks Important?

Free kicks give teams an opportunity to create an attack from a controlled restart.

A talented free-kick taker can put the ball into dangerous areas, while teams can also practice specific routines designed to create scoring opportunities.

Free kicks are especially important in close matches, where one well-executed set piece can change the game.

## Final Thoughts

Free kicks are a fundamental part of football and can be much more interesting than they first appear. Knowing the difference between direct and indirect free kicks makes it easier to understand what is happening when the referee stops play.

Whether it's a powerful shot from outside the penalty area or a carefully planned passing routine, free kicks are an important part of the modern game.
