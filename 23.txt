# What Is a Football Manager? What Does a Manager Do?

Football managers have a major role in how teams prepare for and play matches. They help organize the squad, choose tactics, and make important decisions before and during games.

But what exactly does a football manager do?

## What Is a Football Manager?

A football manager is responsible for leading and organizing a team.

Depending on the country and club, the role may also be called a **head coach**.

The responsibilities can vary between clubs, but managers are generally involved in tactical planning, training, team selection, and match preparation.

## Choosing the Starting Lineup

One of a manager's most visible responsibilities is selecting the starting lineup.

They decide which players begin the match and which players start on the substitutes' bench.

These decisions can depend on:

* Player form
* Injuries
* Suspensions
* Opponent tactics
* Fitness
* Tactical plans

## Creating Tactics

Managers develop tactical plans for their teams.

They may decide how the team should attack, defend, press, build from the back, or transition between defense and attack.

A manager can also change the team's formation depending on the situation.

## Making Substitutions

Managers can make substitutions during matches according to the competition's rules.

A substitution can be used to:

* Replace a tired player
* Respond to an injury
* Change the team's tactics
* Add more attacking options
* Strengthen the defense
* Manage the final stages of a match

## Training and Preparation

Managers and their coaching staff help prepare players for upcoming matches.

Training sessions can include work on:

* Passing
* Finishing
* Defending
* Set pieces
* Fitness
* Tactical organization

The exact training program depends on the team and its schedule.

## Working With the Coaching Staff

A manager usually does not work alone.

Modern football clubs can have large coaching and performance teams, including assistant coaches, fitness staff, analysts, medical professionals, and other specialists.

These staff members help players and coaches prepare for matches and training.

## Scouting Opponents

Managers may study upcoming opponents to understand how they play.

They can look at areas such as:

* Formation
* Attacking patterns
* Defensive organization
* Set pieces
* Key players
* Pressing style

This information can influence the team's match plan.

## Managers and Transfers

At some clubs, managers have significant input into recruitment and transfers.

At other clubs, sporting directors or recruitment departments have larger roles.

The structure varies from one club to another.

Managers may identify positions that need strengthening or recommend players who fit their tactical approach.

## What Happens During a Match?

During a match, managers can communicate with players and coaching staff, monitor how the game is developing, and make tactical adjustments.

They may change formations, make substitutions, or alter the team's approach depending on the score and circumstances.

## Final Thoughts

Football managers are responsible for much more than simply standing on the sideline during matches.

They help prepare teams, develop tactics, select lineups, manage substitutions, and work with coaching staff throughout the season.

Their exact responsibilities depend on the club, league, and structure of the football organization.
