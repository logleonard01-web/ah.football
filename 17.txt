# What Is Extra Time in Football? Rules Explained

Sometimes a football match needs a winner, but the score is still level after the normal 90 minutes. In certain knockout competitions, the match can then go to **extra time**.

Extra time gives both teams another opportunity to score before a potential penalty shootout.

## What Is Extra Time?

Extra time is an additional period played after the normal 90 minutes when a competition requires a winner.

It is normally made up of two **15-minute periods**, giving teams up to 30 additional minutes.

Extra time is different from stoppage time. Stoppage time is added to each half of normal play, while extra time is an additional period played after the original 90 minutes.

## When Is Extra Time Used?

Extra time is generally used in knockout matches where one team must advance.

It is not used in every football match.

For example, a league match can finish as a draw if the competition rules allow it. A knockout match that requires one team to progress may use extra time if the scores are level after normal time.

## How Does Extra Time Work?

The teams play an additional 30 minutes divided into two halves of 15 minutes.

There is a short interval between the two periods.

The team that scores more goals during extra time wins the match, unless the competition has another method for determining the winner.

## Is There a Golden Goal?

Modern football competitions generally do not use the old **golden goal** system.

Under the golden goal rule, the first team to score during extra time immediately won the match.

Today, teams normally play the full extra-time period even if a goal is scored.

## What Happens If the Score Is Still Tied?

If the score remains level after extra time, the competition may use a **penalty shootout** to determine which team advances.

Each team takes penalties according to the competition's procedures, with the winner progressing.

## Extra Time vs. Stoppage Time

These terms are sometimes confused.

**Stoppage time:** Additional time added at the end of a normal half to account for certain delays during play.

**Extra time:** Additional periods played after the normal match when a knockout competition needs to determine a winner.

They are two different things.

## How Do Players Handle Extra Time?

Extra time can be physically demanding because players have already completed 90 minutes.

Teams may make substitutions, adjust their tactics, and manage their energy differently as the match continues.

Defensive organization and concentration can become especially important during the later stages.

## Can Extra Time Change the Game?

Absolutely.

A team that was defending for much of normal time may find another opportunity during extra time, while an attacking team may continue pushing for a decisive goal.

Extra time can also create tactical changes as managers respond to tired players and the changing situation.

## Final Thoughts

Extra time gives teams another chance to settle a tied knockout match before a penalty shootout.

With two 15-minute periods, it can add another 30 minutes of football and often creates some of the most dramatic moments in knockout competitions.
