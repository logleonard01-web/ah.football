# What Is a Football Assist? How Assists Are Recorded

Goals usually receive the most attention in football, but the pass or action that creates a goal can be just as important. This is where the **assist** comes in.

An assist is generally credited to a player whose action directly helps a teammate score, although the exact rules for recording assists can vary between competitions and statistical providers.

## What Is an Assist?

In simple terms, an assist is the final pass or significant attacking action that leads to a teammate scoring a goal.

For example, if a midfielder passes the ball to a striker and the striker immediately scores, the midfielder may be credited with an assist.

Assists help show which players are contributing to goals without actually scoring them.

## The Final Pass

The most straightforward type of assist is a direct pass.

Imagine a winger receives the ball near the side of the penalty area and crosses it to a teammate. If that teammate scores directly from the cross, the winger would commonly receive an assist.

This type of play is one of the easiest assists to identify.

## Assists Can Come in Different Forms

An assist does not always look like a simple pass.

Depending on the statistical rules being used, an assist may come from:

* A through ball
* A cross
* A corner
* A free kick
* A pass into the penalty area
* Another attacking action that directly creates the goal

The exact definition can differ between competitions and statistical providers.

## What If a Defender Touches the Ball?

This is where assist rules can become more complicated.

Suppose a player passes toward a teammate, but a defender touches or slightly changes the ball's path before the teammate scores.

Whether an assist is awarded can depend on the nature of the defensive touch and the rules used by the relevant statistics provider.

A deliberate defensive action and an accidental deflection may be treated differently.

## What About Rebounds?

Rebounds can also create unusual situations.

For example, a player takes a shot that is saved by the goalkeeper, and another teammate scores from the rebound.

Some statistical systems may credit the original shooter with an assist, while others use different criteria.

This is why official competition statistics are useful when checking individual assist totals.

## Can a Player Assist Their Own Goal?

No.

A player cannot receive an assist for a goal they score themselves.

The goal is credited to the scorer, while an eligible teammate may receive the assist.

## Why Are Assists Important?

Goals and assists provide different information about attacking contributions.

A player might score frequently, while another player creates many of those opportunities.

Looking at both statistics can provide a broader picture of how players contribute to their team's attacking play.

However, assists do not capture every useful action. A player can create space, make an important pass earlier in an attack, or start a move without receiving an official assist.

## Assists in Football Statistics

Assist totals are commonly included in player statistics across leagues and competitions.

They can also appear in fantasy football, where assists may provide additional points depending on the game's scoring system.

Because definitions can differ, it is important to check which statistical provider or competition is being used when comparing assist totals.

## Final Thoughts

An assist recognizes a player's contribution to a goal without being the player who scores it.

While the final pass is the most familiar example, different situations can make assist decisions more complicated. Understanding the rules behind assists can make player statistics and match analysis easier to follow.
